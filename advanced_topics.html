Advanced Topics in Folklore Studies

Learners who have mastered the foundational genres, history, and field documentation methods of Folklore Studies can specialize in specific subdisciplines. These five tracks explore applied cultural policy, vernacular belief systems, digital participatory cultures, indigenous and diasporic epistemologies, and the linguistics of oral performance.

Specialization Tracks

  • Public Folklore, Cultural Policy, and Applied Folkloristics: Focuses on cultural conservation, intangible heritage governance, museum ethnography, and community advocacy.
  • Vernacular Religion, Belief Systems, and Contemporary Legend: Explores lived religious expressions, the experiential source hypothesis, contemporary legend dynamics, and collective anxieties.
  • Digital Folkloristics, Algorithmic Vernaculars, and Media Ecology: Investigates networked vernacular cultures, internet memetics, online antagonism, and the folkloric dynamics of platforms.
  • Indigenous Epistemologies, Decoloniality, and Diasporic Traditions: Examines sovereign indigenous narrative systems, decolonial research paradigms, and African diasporic cultural retention.
  • Ethnopoetics, Sociolinguistics, and Expressive Performance: Analyzes the poetic architecture of spoken narrative, the ethnography of speaking, language ideologies, and reflexive performance framing.

Public Folklore, Cultural Policy, and Applied Folkloristics

Explores the application of folkloristic expertise to cultural conservation, public programming, heritage policy, and community advocacy outside the academy.

Intangible Cultural Heritage and Sustainable Development: Living Heritage and Sustainable Development (UNESCO / SDG Academy / edX) - An online course covering the 2003 UNESCO Convention, participatory safeguarding, and the integration of living traditions into community development.

Public Sector Folklore and Cultural Conservation: Public Folklore (Robert Baron and Nicholas R. Spitzer, eds. / Internet Archive) - A seminal collection examining the role of folklorists in state arts agencies, national parks, and public festivals, addressing representation, advocacy, and cultural conservation.

Critical Heritage Studies and Display Politics: Destination Culture: Tourism, Museums, and Heritage (Barbara Kirshenblatt-Gimblett / Internet Archive) - A critical text analyzing how cultural performance, ethnographic objects, and living heritage are produced, staged, and consumed in museum exhibitions and tourist economies.

Collaborative Curation and Environmental Folklife: Conserving Culture: A New Approach to Heritage Planning (Mary Hufford, ed.) - Examines cultural conservation as an ecosystemic practice, demonstrating how folklorists collaborate with communities to safeguard cultural landscapes and traditional livelihoods.

Vernacular Religion, Belief Systems, and Contemporary Legend

Investigates lived religious expression, supernatural assault experiences, contemporary legend dynamics, and the dialectics of belief in everyday contexts.

The Experiential Source Hypothesis: The Terror That Comes in the Night: An Experience-Centered Study of Supernatural Assault Traditions (David J. Hufford / Internet Archive) - A landmark phenomenological study establishing that supernatural assault traditions often derive from cross-culturally consistent sleep paralysis events rather than cultural conditioning alone.

Vernacular Religion and Lived Belief: "Vernacular Religion and the Search for Method in Religious Folklife" (Leonard Norman Primiano, Western Folklore) - A groundbreaking theoretical essay redefining folk religion as lived, idiosyncratic belief, shifting scholarly focus from institutional dogma to individual religious practice.

Contemporary Legend and Rumor Epistemologies: Legend and Belief: Dialectics of a Folklore Genre (Linda Dégh) - An exhaustive investigation into how contemporary legends circulate, debate contested truths, and articulate collective cultural anxieties.

Ostension and Ghost Hunting in Popular Culture: Haunting Experiences: Ghosts in Contemporary Folklore (Diane E. Goldstein, Sylvia Ann Grider, and Jeannie Banks Thomas / Utah State University Press) - An open-access study examining contemporary ghostlore, paranormal investigation subcultures, and the commodification of supernatural narratives in modern mass media.

Digital Folkloristics, Algorithmic Vernaculars, and Media Ecology

Analyzes how networked digital technologies, algorithmic sorting, and participatory media platforms reshape folkloric transmission, memetics, and vernacular authority.

Algorithmic Vernaculars and Platform Folklore: Tradition in the Twenty-First Century: Locating the Folk in a Digital Age (Trevor J. Blank and Robert Glenn Howard, eds.) - Investigates how traditional folklore processes adapt to digital ecosystems, analyzing vernacular authority, digital performance, and mediated expressive culture.

Digital Antagonism and Vernacular Play: The Ambivalent Internet: Mischief, Oddity, and Antagonism Online (Whitney Phillips and Ryan M. Milner) - Examines memetics, trolling, and digital humor to demonstrate how vernacular online expression generates both playful solidarity and targeted harm.

Memetics and Participatory Culture: Memes in Digital Culture (Limor Shifman) - An analytical framework for understanding internet memes as dynamic cultural artifacts governed by imitation, variation, and participatory user engagement.

Digital Archaeology of Early Web Vernaculars: olia lialina: The Only Thing We Know About Cyberspace Is That Its 640x480 (Media.ccc.de / YouTube) - A specialist lecture on early web vernaculars, amateur homepages, and the methodology of preserving born-digital folklore.

Indigenous Epistemologies, Decoloniality, and Diasporic Traditions

Explores sovereign indigenous narrative systems, decolonial ethnographic methodologies, and the politics of cultural retention and resistance across diasporic communities.

Indigenous Landscape, Language, and Place-Making: Wisdom Sits in Places: Landscape and Language Among the Western Apache (Keith H. Basso) - An ethnography illustrating how Apache geographical names anchor ancestral moral narratives, cultural history, and community identity to physical landscapes.

Decolonial Methodologies and Cultural Sovereignty: Decolonizing Methodologies: Research and Indigenous Peoples (Linda Tuhiwai Smith / Internet Archive) - A foundational critique of colonial extraction in ethnographic collecting, offering decolonial research paradigms and indigenous intellectual property protections.

Indigenous Worldviews and Oral Traditions: Indigenous Canada (University of Alberta / Coursera) - A university course exploring indigenous worldviews, oral narratives, and contemporary political resistance from First Nations, Métis, and Inuit perspectives.

African Diasporic Folklore, Creolization, and Resistance: The Man-of-Words in the West Indies: Performance and the Communication of Poetics (Roger D. Abrahams / Internet Archive) - An ethnographic study of Afro-Caribbean verbal art, exploring how oral rhetoric, performative competence, and creolization operate as acts of cultural resistance.

Ethnopoetics, Sociolinguistics, and Expressive Performance

Investigates the poetic architecture of spoken narrative, the sociolinguistics of vernacular registers, and performance-centered approaches to communicative events.

Structural Ethnopoetics and Oral Verse Transcription: "In Vain I Tried to Tell You": Essays in Native American Ethnopoetics (Dell Hymes / Internet Archive) - Foundational essays formulating structural ethnopoetics, showing how oral narratives are organized into measured lines and verses rather than prose paragraphs.

Structural Ethnopoetics and Oral Verse Transcription: The Spoken Word and the Work of Interpretation (Dennis Tedlock / Internet Archive) - Demonstrates acoustic and phenomenological transcription methods, capturing pauses, intonations, and audible breath in live oral performances.

The Ethnography of Speaking and Vernacular Interaction: Ethnography of Speaking (NPTEL / Swayam / IIT Guwahati) - A comprehensive university course exploring language as social action, communicative competence, speech acts, and sociolinguistic variation across cultural communities.

Language Ideologies and the Politics of Representation: Voices of Modernity: Language Ideologies and the Politics of Inequality (Richard Bauman and Charles L. Briggs / Internet Archive) - Analyzes how historical language ideologies in folklore collection constructed the "traditional" other and reinforced social inequality.

Contextual Event Analysis and Metanarration: Story, Performance, and Event: Contextual Studies of Oral Narrative (Richard Bauman) - An advanced performance-theory study examining how storytellers contextualize events, manage audience expectations, and reflexively navigate narrative framing.
